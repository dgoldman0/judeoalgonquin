## 1. Overview
Judeo-Algonquin (also: Munsee–Evrit) is a fusion conlang designed as a “child language” of:
- **Hebrew** (as a Judeo-language superstratum): writing tradition, many function words, discourse style, some derivational patterns.
- **Munsee/Lunáapeew (Delaware)** and broader **Eastern Algonquian** features (as a substrate): core verb/noun morphology, polysynthetic tendencies, locatives, person marking, and categories such as (optional) animacy.

The intended result is not “Hebrew with a few Algonquian nouns,” and not “Munsee with Hebrew script.” Instead, the language is built so that everyday clauses are typically driven by **Algonquian-style predicates and person marking**, while **Hebrew supplies connective tissue** (articles, conjunctions, pragmatic particles) and a Judeo-literary register.

---

## 2. Writing System (Hebrew Script for Algonquian Phonology)

### 2.1 Orthographic principle
Hebrew script is used as an **abjad-plus-matres** system. Consonants are written normally; vowels are indicated by:
- **Matres lectionis** (י, ו, א) in running text.
- Optional **niqqud** in pedagogical/ritual contexts or dictionaries.

### 2.2 Recommended phoneme mapping (practical)
A workable Judeo-Algonquin spelling system:
- /w/ → ו
- /y/ (/j/) → י
- /š/ → שׁ
- /č/ (if present in chosen Algonquian variety) → צ׳ or טש
- /h/ → ה
- /θ/ (if ever introduced) → ת׳ (rare; optional)
- Long vowels may be marked by doubled mater (e.g., וּו /oo/) or by a geresh-like convention.

### 2.3 Morpheme boundary visibility
Because Algonquian morphology is dense, optional boundary markers can appear in careful writing:
- Maqaf (־) for clitic groups or derivational stacks.
- Apostrophe/geresh (׳) for morpheme breaks in didactic text.

Example (didactic):
- ב־וִיקְוָאהְמֶנְק  **b-wi·kwáhm-ənk**  “in-house”
- נֶ־מִּיצוּ  **nə-miitsuw**  “we eat”

---

## 3. Lexicon and Borrowing Strategy

### 3.1 Functional split
- **Hebrew-origin**: articles, conjunctions, prepositions (as clitics), discourse particles, many abstract nouns in higher registers.
  - Typical: ה־, ו־, ב־, ל־, שׁ־, גם, רק, אבל, הנה, אולי, כבר
- **Munsee/Lenape/Algonquian-origin**: core everyday verbs, many concrete nouns, spatial terms, kinship terms, common adjectives/statives.

### 3.2 Borrowing constraints
1. Borrowing targets **semantic domains that remain culturally grounded**:
   - land/seasonality, kin relations, subsistence, motion, perception, making/repairing, local ecology.
2. Borrowed roots remain **morphologically active**:
   - Algonquian roots continue to take Algonquian affixes (person, locative, diminutive).
3. Hebrew loans entering the Algonquian morphological system are **stem-adapted** (see §5.4).

### 3.3 Calquing
A major mechanism is calquing Hebrew idioms into Algonquian predicate structures:
- Hebrew discourse frame + Algonquian predicate:
  - “והנה + [Algonquian verb complex]” (literary “and behold” with Algonquian event encoding)

---

## 4. Core Typological Shift vs. Standard Hebrew

### 4.1 Predicate-centered grammar
Standard Hebrew frequently organizes clauses around:
- verbal templates (binyanim) with tense-aspect expressed in conjugation,
- copular and nominal clauses,
- relatively analytic prepositions.

Judeo-Algonquin shifts toward:
- **predicate-first** clauses where the predicate (often Algonquian) encodes person/number and sometimes object relations.
- fewer copular clauses; more “stative verbs” for qualities and states.

### 4.2 Polysynthesis (moderated)
A defining Algonquian trait is polysynthetic verb complexity. Judeo-Algonquin preserves this tendency but often **moderates** it for readability:
- common everyday predicates may still carry person marking and some derivation,
- extremely long verb complexes may be stylistically restricted to oral/poetic registers.

---

## 5. Morphology: How Word Construction Changes

### 5.1 Person marking: prefixes and agreement
Judeo-Algonquin adopts Algonquian-style person marking where feasible:
- **nə-** 1st person (often “I/we” depending on paradigm)
- **kə-** 2nd person
- **wə-** 3rd person

These appear on:
- verbs (subject agreement; optionally object relations),
- possessed nouns (my/your/his).

This differs sharply from Hebrew, where possession is commonly:
- construct state (סמיכות), suffix pronouns, or של- phrases.

### 5.2 Locatives instead of Hebrew prepositions
Algonquian locatives (e.g., **-ənk**) are productive and reduce reliance on Hebrew prepositions:
- Hebrew: בַּבַּיִת “in the house”
- Judeo-Algonquin: ב־וִיקְוָאהְמֶנְק  “in-house” (b- + wi·kwáhm-ənk)

This yields a double system:
- Hebrew clitic ב־ introduces a phrase (discourse/conventional),
- Algonquian locative encodes the actual spatial relation.

### 5.3 Diminutives and derivation
Algonquian diminutives (e.g., **-əš**) remain active:
- “bear” → “little bear” via -əš
Hebrew diminutives are not systematic; Judeo-Algonquin therefore shifts affective nuance toward Algonquian morphology.

### 5.4 Hebrew stems entering Algonquian morphology
Hebrew lexical items can be “Algonquianized” to become stems that accept Algonquian affixes:
- Hebrew root enters as a phonological stem (often with a vowel scaffold),
- then takes Algonquian person/locative/derivational morphology.

Example (schematic):
- סֵפֶר “book” → stem **סֵפַר-** (sefar-) + locative **-ənk** → **סֵפַרֶנְק** “at/in the book (place/context)” (register-dependent; not obligatory)

This differs from Hebrew’s template morphology: Judeo-Algonquin derivation is frequently **affixal stacking** rather than binyan templating.

### 5.5 Animacy (optional but recommended)
To honor Algonquian structure more deeply, Judeo-Algonquin may implement **animacy classes**:
- nouns lexically specified as Animate or Inanimate,
- affecting plural marking and agreement patterns.

Hebrew has grammatical gender (masc/fem), not animacy. Judeo-Algonquin can:
- retain Hebrew gender in Hebrew-origin nouns,
- use Algonquian animacy in Algonquian-origin nouns,
- allow a mapping layer (e.g., many Hebrew fem nouns may be treated as Inanimate unless culturally “alive”).

### 5.6 Direct–Inverse (optional, simplified)
Algonquian languages often mark whether the action proceeds from a higher-ranked participant (1/2 > 3) or inverse. Judeo-Algonquin can:
- preserve a **reduced direct/inverse** marker set in the verb,
- or express it analytically using Hebrew particles plus a stable verb form.

Because direct–inverse is complex, many implementations keep it for formal style and simplify it in everyday speech.

---

## 6. Syntax: Clause Structure and Word Order

### 6.1 Flexible order with verb-heavy predicates
Algonquian languages allow flexible order due to rich morphology. Judeo-Algonquin tends toward:
- Predicate-initial or predicate-medial clauses,
- topicalization with Hebrew particles (ו־, והנה, הרי, גם).

### 6.2 Hebrew-style clitic chaining
Even when the predicate is Algonquian, Hebrew clitics chain around it:
- ו־ (and) + ב־ (in) + [Algonquian locative form]
- ש־ as a relativizer introduces subordinate clauses while the embedded predicate retains Algonquian morphology.

### 6.3 Relative clauses and complementizers
Hebrew provides compact relativization:
- ש־ + clause
The embedded verb can remain Algonquian-marked, producing a hybrid where Hebrew handles subordination and Algonquian handles event/person encoding.

---

## 7. Tense, Aspect, and Modality

### 7.1 Shift from Hebrew tense to aspectual framing
Modern Hebrew typically encodes tense through conjugation (past/present/future). Judeo-Algonquin leans toward:
- aspectual particles (Hebrew already has aspectual adverbs/particles: כבר, עדיין, עוד, תמיד),
- Algonquian-like event framing (ongoing vs. completed vs. habitual) expressed through verbal derivation or particles.

### 7.2 Register split
- **Colloquial**: Hebrew particles + simplified Algonquian verb forms.
- **High/ritual/poetic**: fuller Algonquian derivational machinery (and optional direct/inverse).

## 8. Sample Constructions (illustrative patterns)

### 8.1 Locative phrase
- ב־וִיקְוָאהְמֶנְק  
  “in-house”  
  Structure: Hebrew clitic ב־ + Algonquian noun + locative -ənk.

### 8.2 Person-marked predicate with Hebrew framing
- וְנֶמִּיצוּ אֲפוּאָן  
  “and we eat bread”  
  Structure: Hebrew conjunction ו־ + Algonquian person-marked verb + Algonquian noun object.

### 8.3 Discourse particle + Algonquian event encoding
- הִנֵּה נוֹפּוּנְדַאוּוִין  
  “behold, (there is) hearing / we are hearing” (interpretation depends on chosen paradigm)  
  Hebrew discourse particle sets tone; Algonquian root carries event semantics.

## 9. How It Differs from Standard Hebrew (Summary)

### 9.1 Major departures
1. **Person marking migrates onto predicates and possessed nouns** via Algonquian prefixes (nə-, kə-, wə-), not primarily via Hebrew conjugation or construct state.
2. **Locatives become morphological** (-ənk) rather than relying only on Hebrew prepositions.
3. **Predicate-centered clauses** reduce copular/nominal clause dominance.
4. **Derivation becomes affix-stacking** (Algonquian style) rather than binyan templating as the main engine.
5. **Optional animacy and (optional) direct–inverse** introduce categories absent in Hebrew’s core grammar.

### 9.2 What remains recognizably Hebrew
1. **Script and orthographic culture** (including optional niqqud usage).
2. **Clitic chaining and discourse particles** (ו־, ה־, ב־, ל־, ש־; also הנה/הרי/גם).
3. **Higher-register vocabulary** and literary cadence patterns (parallelism, repetition, chiasmus), now hosting Algonquian predicates.

---

## 10. How It Incorporates Munsee/Lunáapeew/Algonquian (Summary)

### 10.1 Core incorporations
- **Algonquian roots** as everyday semantic backbone.
- **Algonquian morphology** for:
  - person marking (prefixes),
  - locatives (-ənk),
  - diminutives (-əš),
  - (optional) animacy,
  - (optional) direct–inverse.
- **Polysynthetic tendency**: ability to encode rich event information inside predicate complexes.

### 10.2 Cultural-linguistic integration
- Judeo-language continuity: Hebrew writing tradition and discourse registers.
- Algonquian continuity: land/seasonal worldview, motion/perception verbs, relational morphology, and community-centered predicates.

---

## 11. Design Notes for Consistency
1. Hebrew function words should not overwrite Algonquian predicate logic; they should frame it.
2. Algonquian roots should remain morphologically productive; otherwise the result collapses into Hebrew with exotic lexemes.
3. Any “Hebrew-izing” of Algonquian stems should be orthographic/phonological first, not syntactic domination.
4. Registers should exist:
   - everyday spoken hybrid,
   - careful written hybrid,
   - ritual/poetic high register where fuller Algonquian complexity can reappear.

---

## 12. Standalone Glossing Conventions (recommended)
- Hyphen (-) separates morphemes in analysis.
- Prefixes shown left of stem; suffixes right.
- LOC = locative (-ənk), DIM = diminutive (-əš), 1 = first person, 2 = second, 3 = third, PL = plural.

Example (analysis line, not orthography):
- b-wi·kwáhm-ənk  = in-house-LOC
- nə-miitsuw       = 1-eat
