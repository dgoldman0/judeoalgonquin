# Hybrid Narrative Engine

**Authority:** This document specializes the accepted narrative goal in [Language Goals](language-goals.md). It does not independently canonize any form.

**Status:** The combined capability is accepted. Its morphology and detailed distribution are proposed pending source research, construction records, and compositional tests.

## 1. Expressive objective

Judeo-Algonquin should be able to tell a fast story with several active participants while making three things recoverable:

1. which clauses advance the foregrounded event sequence;
2. who is central, who is less central, and when that center changes; and
3. when a particular incident becomes a consequence, recurring response, standing expectation, or remembered custom.

The design achieves this through the interaction of two deliberately adapted Biblical Hebrew narrative resources and an Algonquian-inspired participant system. Merely placing all three somewhere in the grammar does not satisfy the goal; they must function together in connected narrative.

## 2. The three components

### Wayyiqtol-derived foreground chain

The conlang should develop a construction inspired by Biblical Hebrew wayyiqtol for successive foreground events. In its intended project function, each linked clause normally advances narrative reference time and produces the driving, cumulative movement heard in *When the Lights Learn Our Names*.

The eventual construction record must define its form, predicate restrictions, interaction with negation and subordination, permissible interruptions, resumption, and relationship to ordinary tense and aspect. “Wayyiqtol-derived” identifies the design lineage; it does not assert that the adapted rule exhaustively reproduces Biblical Hebrew.

### Weqatal-derived consequence or recurring frame

The conlang should develop a construction inspired by Biblical Hebrew weqatal that can mark a designed transition away from the one-time foreground chain. Its project functions may include prospective consequences, recurring responses, standing expectations, and enduring patterns arising from the narrated event.

The anchor song's final movement—roughly, “thereafter, whenever the lights tremble, they will respond and laugh”—is the key capability test. The final source study must distinguish actual Biblical Hebrew evidence from the conlang's chosen adaptation.

### Proximate/obviative participant tracking

An Algonquian-inspired system should track a discourse center (proximate) against other salient third persons (obviative) across the chained clauses. The system must specify nominal and verbal marking, agreement, scope, center shifts, reintroduction, and interaction with overt names or noun phrases.

A binary proximate/obviative contrast does not uniquely distinguish an unlimited number of participants. Scenes with several obviatives must use additional agreement, lexical anchors, ordering constraints, indexing, or another explicit solution. Ambiguity is a failed test, not evidence that the reader should guess.

The current anchor ledger makes the failure concrete: the final tableau cannot
recover Mara, Jules, and Tina from one proximate label plus two undifferentiated
obviatives. Any analysis that returns only PROX/OBV/OBV fails before stylistic
evaluation. The eventual solution may use agreement, indices, strategic
reanchoring, or constrained repetition, but it must survive blind participant
recovery.

## 3. Required interaction

For every narrative-engine example, the analysis must show both tracks:

- **event track:** whether the clause advances the foreground chain, supplies background, or enters the weqatal-derived consequence/recurring frame;
- **participant track:** the identity and discourse status of every argument, including any center shift.

The intended result is narrative propulsion without sacrificing reference. Wayyiqtol-derived chaining answers “what happened next?”; participant tracking answers “who is doing it to whom?”; the weqatal-derived shift answers “what follows or becomes customary because this happened?”

## 4. Development gates

The engine becomes canonical only through construction records and staged tests:

1. establish sourced descriptions of the donor constructions and participant categories;
2. state the conlang adaptations independently and give complete forms;
3. test each component in isolation;
4. test short chains with two third-person participants;
5. test center shifts and more than one obviative;
6. test a chain-to-consequence or chain-to-recurring-pattern transition;
7. test interruption, background information, dialogue, and resumption; and
8. revise *When the Lights Learn Our Names* with a clause-by-clause event and participant ledger.

Tests must include deliberately ambiguous negative cases. A generated example cannot validate the rule that generated it.

## 5. Relationship to the creative anchor

[*When the Lights Learn Our Names*](../references/creative-anchors/when-the-lights-learn-our-names.md) is the primary creative anchor for this engine. Its humor, modern setting, rapid event chain, final habitual coda, and desire to avoid repetitive renaming are continuity requirements. Its current forms remain editable because the final system must be more rigorous than the legacy analysis.

The goal is therefore neither to freeze the existing translation nor to replace it with generic “Biblical-sounding” prose. The goal is to build the explicit hybrid grammar that the song was reaching for.

The ordinary contact AI plural construction created for *We Walk Well* is not
part of this engine. It establishes transparent inclusive/exclusive participant
marking in independent affirmative predicates; wayyiqtol-derived foreground
linking, weqatal-derived recurrence, obviation, and their interactions remain
separate D1/D2 construction work.

The later ordinary finite-coordination candidate is also outside this engine.
Its Hebrew-derived `wə-` remains proclitic on the second clause and means only
ordinary “and.” It cannot supply succession, consequence, recurrence,
foregrounding, or a discourse-center shift; using it as a substitute for the
wayyiqtol–weqatal–obviation system is an explicit failed analysis.

The seven-way ordinary AI location/posture microparadigm is outside this engine
as well. It expands person, number, and clusivity for `apii-`, `ləmatapii-`, and
`niipawii-`, but its third-person cells do not distinguish proximate from
obviative participants. It supplies no foreground chain, consequence or
recurring frame, discourse-center shift, tense, aspect, or participant-recovery
mechanism.
