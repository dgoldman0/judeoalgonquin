# Language Goals

**Authority:** Highest. If another project document conflicts with this one, this document controls until an explicit decision amends it.

**Status:** Adopted foundation.

## 1. What the language is

Judeo-Algonquin is an intentionally constructed language with two full cultural and linguistic parents.

Its explicit alternate-history premise is that Judea does not fall. A culturally rich Judean community continues to develop, crosses the Atlantic, and settles in the Hudson/Mahicanituck Valley. Sustained contact with the peoples of the valley produces successive contact varieties: a practical pidgin and then a nativized creole, already a full community language, that continues to elaborate across ordinary, intellectual, artistic, communal, and literary life.

Hebrew language and Judean culture are preserved through that history, but not frozen. They change through migration, new ecology, new neighbors, intermarriage, shared institutions, and generations of first-language speakers. Algonquian languages and cultures are equally constitutive of the resulting language. This premise is worldbuilding. It is not undocumented history, a recovered language, a reconstruction, or a claim about the speech of any real Jewish or Indigenous community.

The working project title is **Judeo-Algonquin**. It does not imply that the specific Algonquin language supplies the whole Algonquian layer. Any proposed endonym, including *Djudeo-Mahikanítakh*, remains a transparent project coinage until its components and spelling have been reviewed under the provenance policy.

## 2. Design objective

The language should be a coherent hybrid rather than either of these shallow outcomes:

- ordinary Hebrew with decorative Indigenous words; or
- an Algonquian language mechanically transliterated into Hebrew letters.

The intended architecture is a co-parented language:

- a living Hebrew continuum contributing script, ordinary and specialized lexicon, productive morphology, clause structure, discourse, narrative technique, and cultural continuity;
- source-specific Algonquian lexicon, morphology, clause structure, discourse categories, and cultural continuity that remain productive after adaptation;
- explicit rules explaining how the layers interact; and
- multiple usable registers, from ordinary speech to careful prose and literary or ritual-style composition.

Neither parent is a thin superstrate, substrate, flavoring, or restricted semantic reservoir. A construction may be predominantly Hebrew-derived, predominantly Algonquian-derived, or genuinely hybrid. Its analysis must say which and why.

This allocation is an artistic design choice. It is not a claim that Hebrew is inherently more abstract, literary, ethical, or spiritual, or that Algonquian languages are inherently more concrete, ecological, or physical. Every source language can express the full range of human thought.

## 3. Source hierarchy

The Algonquian side uses a regional anchor followed by a documented gap-filling path:

1. **Regional anchor:** Munsee Delaware / Lunaape material, especially community-connected and speaker-based sources.
2. **First gap-filling search:** responsibly sourced Mahican/Mohican and Lenape material, with the precise language or lect stated.
3. **Broader gap filling:** explicitly labeled Algonquin, Ojibwe, Mi'kmaq, Penobscot, or other Algonquian material may fill a real lexical or design gap when the regional sources are insufficient.

Every move away from the regional anchor requires a recorded gap, a design rationale, and exact attribution. “Algonquian” alone is not sufficient lexical provenance. Related languages are not interchangeable dialect spellings, and a borrowed feature never becomes falsely “Munsee” through adaptation.

For Hebrew, records must distinguish the relevant stratum or register:

- Modern/common Hebrew as important material for ordinary life, productive word formation, connective structure, and contemporary concepts;
- Biblical and Classical Hebrew as important material for narrative, literary construction, inherited morphology, and elevated registers;
- rabbinic, liturgical, regional, or diaspora material when specifically labeled and sourced.

Hebrew-derived vocabulary and morphology may occur across semantic domains. Hebrew is not confined to abstractions, function words, or high style, just as Algonquian-derived material is not confined to land, nature, or concrete life.

## 4. Desired language capacities

The completed language should support:

- ordinary conversation and practical description;
- precise translation in both directions;
- concise predicate-centered clauses;
- productive word formation rather than a memorized phrasebook;
- explicit participant and discourse tracking where the final grammar supports it;
- fast multi-participant narrative in which temporal progression and discourse-center tracking reinforce one another;
- community, lyrical, and literary expression;
- rich compound and subordinate sentences; and
- coherent multi-sentence paragraphs and longer prose.

It should also make the fictional Judean community's cultural continuity legible in ordinary life, names, institutions, family language, memory, humor, learning, law, craft, food, ritual-style composition, and literature—not only in script or elevated quotations.

Algonquian-inspired animacy, direct/inverse alignment, locative morphology, participant marking, and polysynthetic structure are design targets, not inherited facts. Each becomes normative only after its exact source, adaptation, paradigm, and interactions are documented.

### The hybrid narrative engine

The language has one especially important fixed expressive goal: combine adapted Biblical Hebrew **wayyiqtol** and **weqatal** chaining with an Algonquian-inspired **proximate/obviative** participant system.

- A wayyiqtol-derived construction should drive a foregrounded sequence of completed narrative events and advance the story's reference time.
- A weqatal-derived construction should support the designed shift from a particular event chain into prospective consequences, recurring responses, standing expectations, or enduring patterns.
- Proximate/obviative structure should track the current discourse center and other salient third persons across those chains, including explicit and testable rules for center shifts and for scenes containing more than one obviative participant.
- Their interaction—not merely the presence of three unrelated features—should enable rapid multi-participant narration with less repetitive renaming, controlled changes of attention, and a marked transition from event to consequence or remembered custom.

This combined capability is an accepted design requirement. The exact affixes, conjugations, agreement, distribution, and center-shift mechanics remain proposed until they are sourced, adapted coherently, and tested. The project description is a conlang rule, not a claim that Biblical Hebrew or any Algonquian source language independently has the combined system. See [Hybrid Narrative Engine](narrative-engine.md).

## 5. Creative anchors

[*We Walk Well* and *When the Lights Learn Our Names*](../references/creative-anchors/README.md) are active creative anchors.

- *We Walk Well* anchors communal warmth, work, interdependence, hope, song, and a lyrical or liturgical-style voice.
- *When the Lights Learn Our Names* anchors fast narrative movement, humor, modern life, and the specific wayyiqtol–weqatal–obviation synthesis described above.

Their themes, voice, imagery, narrative ambitions, and existing wording are continuity constraints and revision input. Their individual spellings, translations, analyses, and grammatical forms remain reviewable and are not canonical merely because they predate the structured corpus.

The executable continuity ledgers in `data/creative-anchors/` make this an
operational requirement. Before an epoch selects vocabulary or grammar, it must
inspect the relevant song segments and protected functions. After construction,
it must revision-pin any usable candidates, record remaining gaps, and state why
an attractive legacy form was retained, adapted, deferred, or rejected. The
ledger guides design; it never becomes linguistic source evidence.

## 6. Writing-system goals

Hebrew script is a defining representation of the language. The project will also maintain a canonical romanization and explicit morphological analysis so that neither Hebrew-script fluency nor font behavior is required to inspect a record.

The final orthography must:

- represent every accepted phonemic contrast consistently;
- define vowel length, reduced vowels, and consonants absent from standard Hebrew;
- define pointed and unpointed forms;
- use Unicode-normalized text;
- separate orthographic hyphenation from analytical morpheme boundaries; and
- provide deterministic conversion rules where conversion is claimed.

No legacy spelling is grandfathered in merely because it appeared often in generated data.

## 7. Cultural and historical integrity

The project values mutual respect, internal coherence, and careful attribution. In practice this means:

- use community and speaker-connected sources where possible;
- do not collapse distinct nations, languages, or ceremonial systems into a generic Indigenous aesthetic;
- do not invent historical contact, Indigenous beliefs, Jewish law, or shared ceremonies and state them as fact;
- do not treat sacred or community-specific vocabulary as a neutral word bank;
- distinguish a sourced historical origin from a newly constructed formation; and
- explain design choices plainly, without defensiveness or false claims of antiquity.

The language may be openly designed. A candid formation rationale is a strength, not a defect.

## 8. Canon and revision

Canonical status is assigned only by a human reviewer. Active language
candidates are authored through direct linguistic reasoning and explicit
review. Scripts, similarity searches, and consistency checks may retrieve,
validate, test, or recommend revisions, but they do not choose language content
and cannot promote anything to canon.

Canon is revisioned rather than treated as infallible. When later work reveals a conflict, the project records the correction, its reason, its affected dependents, and any superseded form. Earlier reasoning remains auditable.

## 9. Development shape

Development is a spiral of increasing coverage and consistency:

1. build a level-appropriate vocabulary set;
2. compose phrases from that vocabulary;
3. compose sentences from accepted words, constructions, and phrases;
4. retrieve related records and test for conflicts;
5. revise and re-review affected records; and
6. advance to the next level.

Paragraph work begins only after rich sentence composition passes the gates in the roadmap. Embeddings are built and refreshed throughout this process, but the structured records remain authoritative.

## 10. Success criteria

The project succeeds when it produces a source-aware, internally consistent, explainable translation knowledge base—not when it merely produces plausible-looking strings. Coverage, compositionality, provenance, reproducibility, and honest revision matter more than output volume.
