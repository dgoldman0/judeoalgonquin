# Perception and Ordinary Coordination Tranche

**Status:** Manually designed, source-bounded candidate work. Nothing here is
canonical. This tranche deliberately stops short of claiming that contact
“hear” already means attentive “listen,” or that a transitive perception verb
may occur without an object.

## Why this is the next tranche

The first chorus tranche established a real contact word, an AI first-plural
system, and a complete candidate clause. The next unresolved chorus line asks
for two balanced perceptions: “We listen, we see.” The reviewed regional
material, however, does not contain two loose objectless verbs. It contains
three TA/TI pairs whose final selects a grammatically animate or inanimate
object:

| Function | Animate-object stem | Inanimate-object stem |
|---|---|---|
| see | `nee-w-` | `nee-m-` |
| hear | `pənt-aw-` | `pənt-am-` |
| look at | `pən-aw-` | `pən-am-` |

The language therefore needs to learn to use these pairs honestly before it
can decide how to suppress, generalize, or background an object.

## Source findings

O'Meara presents the three pairs at p. 67, §2.1.1–2.1.2, examples 2.6–2.7.
The gloss of `pənt-` at that locator is “hear,” not “listen,” “understand,”
“obey,” or “learn.” `pən-` “look at” remains distinct from `nee-` “see.”

At pp. 86–90, §2.2.0, examples 2.37, 2.39, and 2.43, O'Meara distinguishes
Objective and Absolute independent transitive constructions. In the Absolute:

- the transitive verb takes the intransitive independent inflectional set;
- an overt direct-object noun phrase is obligatory; and
- that object is interpreted as indefinite.

His two displayed first-plural Absolute examples show a TA predicate ending in
`-ahna` before an animate-class indefinite object and a TI predicate ending in
`-ohna` before an inanimate-class indefinite object. Those examples establish
the structural model and the overt-indefinite-object requirement. They do not
by themselves attest every cell coined below.

For the Hebrew co-parent, the Academy of the Hebrew Language lists `קוֹל` as
“sound” in its Physics: Acoustics terminology (1985), while separately listing
human or animal voice as another use. This tranche isolates physical audible
sound. The ordinary contact filter maps source /q/ to `k`, yielding contact
`kol`. Voice, vote, grammatical voice, musical part, reputation, and
metaphorical “voice” are excluded.

## Contact lexical decisions

The six regional stems enter the contact lexicon without collapsing their
TA/TI contrast. Their contact spelling preserves the reviewed root and final:

- `nee-w-` “see an animate-class entity”;
- `nee-m-` “see an inanimate-class entity”;
- `pənt-aw-` “hear an animate-class entity”;
- `pənt-am-` “hear an inanimate-class entity”;
- `pən-aw-` “look at an animate-class entity”; and
- `pən-am-` “look at an inanimate-class entity.”

These remain bound stems. The English placeholders “someone” and “something”
describe class selection; they do not reduce grammatical animacy to biological
or human animacy.

Contact `kol` is a separate direct inheritance from Standard Hebrew `קוֹל` in
the bounded physical-sound sense. The source spelling and broader Hebrew
polysemy remain visible in the donor record, while the contact record stores
the regular contact pronunciation and reversible pointed spelling.

## Limited contact Absolute

The contact construction regularizes a careful first-plural morphography from
the source Absolute examples:

- TA: `PERS~TA-STEM~a~hna NP[AN, indefinite]`;
- TI: `PERS~TI-STEM~o~hna NP[IN, indefinite]`;
- inclusive `PERS` is `kə`, including the addressee; and
- exclusive `PERS` is `nə`, excluding the addressee.

The `a` and `o` are **contact constructional class vowels** abstracted from the
two cited source models. Calling them contact material is important: this pass
does not claim that O'Meara lists them as a general pair of detachable Munsee
morphemes. The output retains every segment of the adopted TA/TI stem and does
not attempt to predict Munsee contraction or surface phonetics.

The twelve admitted predicate cells are:

| Predicate | Class | Inclusive | Exclusive |
|---|---|---|---|
| see | TA | `kəneewahna` | `nəneewahna` |
| see | TI | `kəneemohna` | `nəneemohna` |
| hear | TA | `kəpəntawahna` | `nəpəntawahna` |
| hear | TI | `kəpəntamohna` | `nəpəntamohna` |
| look at | TA | `kəpənawahna` | `nəpənawahna` |
| look at | TI | `kəpənamohna` | `nəpənamohna` |

For this tranche only, `adam` “person” is an admitted animate-class object;
`or` “light,” `kol` “sound,” and `aanay` “road” are admitted inanimate-class
objects. These are construction-specific assignments, not a completed contact
noun-class theory. Hebrew grammatical gender is irrelevant to the assignment.

The construction requires predicate–object order and an overt indefinite
object. It does not license definite objects, omitted objects, singular or
second-person subjects, direct/inverse agreement, obviation, negation,
questions, commands, dependent order, or narrative-chain morphology.

## Phrase and sentence outputs

The nominal phrase `or wə-kol` means “light and sound.” It reuses the ordinary
Hebrew-derived coordinator already tested in nominal coordination, now between
two contact nouns. The coordinator remains a proclitic on the second conjunct;
it is not narrative waw.

The basic contact clauses are:

- `kəneemohna or` — “We, including you, see light.”
- `kəneewahna adam` — “We, including you, see a person.”
- `kəpəntamohna kol` — “We, including you, hear a sound.”
- `kəpəntawahna adam` — “We, including you, hear a person.”
- `kəpənamohna aanay` — “We, including you, look at a road.”
- `kəpənawahna adam` — “We, including you, look at a person.”

A new ordinary finite-coordination construction places proclitic `wə-` on the
second of two complete affirmative clauses whose first-person-plural clusivity
agrees:

- `kəpəntamohna kol wə-kəneemohna or` — “We hear a sound and see light.”
- `kəpəntamohna kol wə-kənaxkoohəmaahna` — “We hear a sound and sing.”

The coordinator contributes coordination only. It contributes no sequence,
consequence, recurrence, tense, aspect, foregrounding, or center shift, and it
must never be used as a cheap substitute for the future
wayyiqtol–weqatal–obviation engine.

## Creative-anchor result and remaining gap

The first coordinated sentence preserves the song's hearing-before-seeing
order, balanced first-plural clauses, and co-parent texture, but it is only
partial continuity: it says literal “hear,” supplies explicit objects, and does
not yet say attentive “listen.” The second sentence gives the bridge an honest
hearing-plus-song prototype.

The eventual compact chorus line still requires a separately declared contact
strategy for attended perception and object backgrounding. That strategy must
be tested against all six class-sensitive stems and must not rewrite the donor
evidence to pretend that the objectless forms were inherited. Meter and owner
lyric review remain later gates.
