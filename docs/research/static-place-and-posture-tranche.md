# Static place and posture tranche

**Status:** manually authored candidate design, not canonical grammar

**Level:** N1 novice enrichment
**Scope:** static location, three animate-intransitive location/posture predicates, ordinary person-number, and static `where` questions

## Why this tranche comes next

The first contact clauses can say that an inclusive or exclusive group walks, sings, sees, hears, or looks at an overt object. They still cannot locate a person, contrast `I`, `you`, `we`, and `they` across an ordinary intransitive paradigm, or ask the basic question `where?`. Those gaps make the corpus broad in isolated nouns but shallow in connected ordinary speech.

This tranche therefore builds one tightly connected semantic field rather than adding an unrelated word list. Hebrew-derived household nouns and a Hebrew-derived static question word enter a regional locative and predicate system. The result is contact-language construction, not alternating Hebrew and Munsee quotations: `bayit` “house” can take regional `-ənk`, and `efo` “where” frames a regionally inflected animate-intransitive predicate.

## Reviewed regional evidence

O'Meara's Moraviantown Munsee grammar provides four bounded pieces of evidence.

1. On p. 49, R19 gives `/aanay-ənk/` “on the road” → `aaneenk`, with `/ay-ə/` contracting to `ee` before a consonant. R20 gives `/mohkaməy-ənk/` “on the ice” → `mohkamiink`, with `/əy-ə/` contracting to `ii` before a consonant. These are exact source examples, not generated outputs.
2. On p. 86, example 2.37 gives the seven independent-order AI person-number patterns: `/nə-/…` `/-m/` first singular, `/kə-/…` `/-m/` second singular, `/-w/` third singular, `/kə-/…` `/-hna/` first plural inclusive, `/nə-/…` `/-hna/` first plural exclusive, `/kə-/…` `/-hmwa/` second plural, and `/-w-ak/` third plural. Footnote 25 defines the inclusive/exclusive contrast.
3. O'Meara gives `/apii-w/` “he is there” on p. 14, `/ləmatapii-w/` “he sits” and `/kə-ləmatapii-m/` “you sit” in the R13 discussion on p. 47, and `/niipaw-ii-w/` “he stands” in the AI-final discussion on p. 133.
4. The same source warns against naïve surface concatenation. In particular,
   R13 produces source `ləmatapəw` from `/ləmatapii-w/`, while prefixes and
   vowel-initial stems may trigger other rules. A source-facing paradigm record
   must therefore distinguish underlying affix patterns from attested surface
   words.

The project records these facts at their cited analytical level. They do not authorize a generic pan-Algonquian locative, a complete Munsee grammar, or automatic transfer of every donor morphophonological rule.

## Contact decisions

### Static locative

The contact language provisionally inherits `-ənk` as one **static/general locative**. Its initial host list is deliberately closed:

- regional `aanay` “road” → `aaneenk`;
- regional `mohkaməy` “ice” → `mohkamiink`;
- regional `apiinay` “bed” → `apiineenk`;
- Hebrew-derived `bayit` “house, home as a dwelling” → `bayitənk`;
- Hebrew-derived `xeder` “room” → `xederənk`; and
- Hebrew-derived `šulxan` “table” → `šulxanənk`.

The two exact source cells retain their cited contractions. `apiineenk` extends the same `-ay + -ənk` contact pattern to an already admitted regional noun; O'Meara's compound `wəšayapiineenk` “at the edge of the bed” corroborates the source boundary but does not attest the standalone contact phrase. O'Meara's `alaamiikwahmənk` “inside the house” also demonstrates a consonant-final source host. The three consonant-final Hebrew-derived nouns therefore take transparent `-ənk` as contact regularizations. This is a deliberately visible co-parent synthesis, not a claim that the forms occur in either donor language.

`-ənk` does not yet distinguish `in`, `on`, and `at`. Idiomatic English translations choose the likely relation for a scene, while literal translations and notes retain `LOC`. The suffix does not mean `to`, `from`, `through`, `toward`, or `away from`; it cannot supply a goal, source, route, or motion event.

### Location and posture predicates

The contact stems are `apii-` “be located; be there,” `ləmatapii-` “sit,” and `niipawii-` “stand.” The construction retains the seven-way regional person architecture but uses transparent contact morphography:

| Participant | Contact pattern | `apii-` example |
|---|---|---|
| first singular | `nə~STEM~m` | `nəapiim` |
| second singular | `kə~STEM~m` | `kəapiim` |
| third singular | `STEM~w` | `apiiw` |
| first plural inclusive | `kə~STEM~hna` | `kəapiihna` |
| first plural exclusive | `nə~STEM~hna` | `nəapiihna` |
| second plural | `kə~STEM~hmwa` | `kəapiihmwa` |
| third plural | `STEM~w~ak` | `apiiwak` |

The same seven cells are written for all three licensed stems. The contact candidate makes `-m` obligatory in first- and second-person singular for these vowel-final stems, preserves full stem vowels, and suspends source contraction, R10 `t`-insertion on vowel-initial `apii-`, W-shift, and deletion. Thus contact `nəapiim` is not source `ntapiim`, and contact `ləmatapiiw` is not source Munsee `ləmatapəw`. These are consciously regularized mixed-language forms whose regional architecture remains recoverable.

The cells index an animate participant but do not yet encode tense, aspect, modality, obviation, or narrative status. English simple present is only a translation convenience. Third singular is the ordinary unmarked/default discourse participant; proximate/obviative contrasts remain for the later narrative and multi-third-person system.

### Clauses and questions

An ordinary static-location clause places a finite AI predicate before the locative phrase. A third-person lexical subject precedes that predicate; first and second persons may be expressed by verbal indexing alone:

- `adam apiiw xederənk` — “A person is in a room.”
- `yeled ləmatapiiw bayitənk` — “A child sits in a house.”
- `kələmatapiihna šulxanənk` — “We, including you, sit at a table.”
- `nələmatapiihna bayitənk` — “We, excluding you, sit in a house.”

The Hebrew-derived contact interrogative is `efo`, restricted to static location. It is clause-initial in this candidate:

- `efo adam apiiw` — “Where is a person?”
- `efo kələmatapiihna` — “Where do we, including you, sit?”

This does not establish a general question syntax, relative `where`, `wherever`, `where to`, or `where from`.

## Ordinary coordination layer repair

Before this repair, current contact phrases and clauses pronounced ordinary
Hebrew-derived `wə-` while some records pointed directly to the source-facing
Hebrew donor morpheme. This tranche creates a separate contact morpheme and
contact nominal-coordination construction and revision-pins current contact
compositions to that layer. The source-facing donor remains intact for evidence
and historical pilot regressions.

This repair changes no surface sentence. It makes the architecture tell the truth: a finished contact composition depends on a contact candidate, while that candidate transparently depends on its Hebrew donor. Ordinary `wə-` remains explicitly separate from wayyiqtol, weqatal, sequencing, consequence, foregrounding, and obviation.

## Creative-anchor continuity

The new material advances both preserved songs without forcing a premature lyric translation.

- *We Walk Well* gains usable contact words for house/home and child, a static road phrase, ordinary full-person AI morphology, and household scenes. These support the song's road, home, communal, and intergenerational domains, but do not yet solve directional “forward,” possession, work predicates, or meter.
- *When the Lights Learn Our Names* gains location questions, default third-person location/posture clauses, and a broader participant system. These are prerequisites for scene staging, but they do not substitute for wayyiqtol, weqatal, obviation, discourse-center shifts, reciprocal action, or the song's full narrative velocity.

The source song texts remain unchanged. Ledger links state partial coverage and unresolved requirements rather than treating lexical overlap as completion.

## Explicit non-goals

This tranche does not license:

- possession or possessive noun paradigms;
- nominal plural, gender, or a complete animate/inanimate noun system;
- negation, existence/nonexistence, imperatives, or polarity questions;
- directed motion, goals, sources, paths, or spatial case contrasts;
- TA/TI extension of the seven-way AI paradigm;
- a general copula or an English-style adjective system;
- obviation or any wayyiqtol/weqatal narrative construction;
- productive attachment of `-ənk` beyond the six recorded hosts; or
- source-attested status for any contact-regularized form.

Every output in this tranche is manually selected and reviewed. Code may validate the declared cells, dependencies, spellings, and semantic firewalls, but it does not choose words or generate grammar.
