# First *We Walk Well* Chorus Construction Tranche

**Status:** Hand-authored, structurally valid candidate work. Nothing in this tranche is canonical, and it is not yet a complete four-line chorus.

## Outcome

This pass moves beyond donor collection and constructs a usable piece of the contact language. It delivers:

- contact lexemes `lew` “heart; inner disposition,” `or` “physical light,” and `aanay` “road”;
- contact AI predicate stems `pəməsii-` “walk,” `wələsii-` “be good,” and `naxkoohəmaa-` “sing”;
- a six-cell inclusive/exclusive first-person-plural AI paradigm;
- the new mixed word `wəlew` “good heart; goodwill”;
- a lexically restricted goodwill manner frame; and
- three composed clauses, culminating in the provisional chorus kernel `wəlew kəpəməsiihna`.

The language decisions were made manually from the source evidence, project goals, and two creative-anchor ledgers. Code validates spelling, references, and dependencies; it did not select the words, meanings, morphology, or clause analysis.

## Source recovery that changes the continuity judgment

O'Meara prints `naxkooh(ə)meew`, analyzes it as `/naxkooh-əm-aa-w/`, and glosses it “he sings” (1990, p. 230, §3.2.3.3, example 3.67). The surrounding discussion says this small class is nonproductive and that forms without a recorded underlying TI stem are listed rather than derived.

That evidence matters because both creative anchors contain legacy `naxkooh...` shapes. The legacy songs are still not source evidence, and their exact `u`, endings, and ring/sing uses remain unsupported. O'Meara supplies the short gloss “he sings”; semantic range beyond that gloss remains unresolved in the donor record. The project provisionally allocates vocal “sing” to contact `naxkoohəmaa-`, while excluding metallic ringing. It must not expose `-əm-aa` as a productive recipe.

O'Meara's independent-order table (p. 86, example 2.37) gives `/kə-/ ... /-hna/` for first-person plural inclusive and `/nə-/ ... /-hna/` for first-person plural exclusive. The clusivity value belongs to the whole discontinuous cell, not to any one affix in isolation.

## Contact person-number decision

The contact candidate retains the regional ordering and clusivity distinction but regularizes a careful morphographic realization:

| Predicate | Inclusive, including the addressee | Exclusive, excluding the addressee |
|---|---|---|
| walk | `kəpəməsiihna` | `nəpəməsiihna` |
| be good | `kəwələsiihna` | `nəwələsiihna` |
| sing | `kənaxkoohəmaahna` | `nənaxkoohəmaahna` |

The analytical patterns are `kə~STEM~hna` and `nə~STEM~hna`. The transparent spellings preserve source schwa and the contact stem's `ii` or `aa` before `-hna`. They deliberately do not import Munsee prefix contraction, schwa deletion, assimilation, or R30 shortening. They are therefore contact morphographic candidates, not predictions of Moraviantown Munsee surface pronunciation.

The construction is restricted to the three tested independent affirmative contact AI stems. It does not apply to other AI hosts, TA or TI stems, possession, negation, imperatives, dependent clauses, or either narrative-chain construction. The six cells form a limited candidate paradigm, not accepted productive grammar. They contribute participant, number, and clusivity, but no tense, aspect, or modality; the English simple present is only a glossing convenience.

## Co-parent formation

The chorus's ethical thesis needs more than a regional predicate with a Hebrew noun placed nearby. The first genuinely mixed chorus word is:

`wəl-lew` → `wəlew`

- contact `wəl-`, manually extracted as a bound positive-quality element from the sourced AI analysis `/wəl-əsii-/`;
- Hebrew-derived contact `lew`, in its separately sourced “inner disposition / moral character” sense; and
- a word-specific reduction of boundary `/l-l/` to `/l/`.

The output is a right-headed abstract noun meaning “good heart; goodwill,” specifically a benevolent inner disposition. It does not mean merely cheerful, and it is not evidence for productive identical-consonant reduction or a general `wəl- + noun` compound rule.

The separate `wəlew PREDICATE` construction lets this one noun frame the disposition in which one of the licensed first-person-plural AI actions is performed. It is not a generic bare-noun adverb rule or a general finite-AI construction. It does not invent plural hearts, possession, a comitative marker, or a free English-style adjective.

## First composed clauses

### `kəpəməsiihna`

- analysis: `kə~pəməsii~hna`
- literal: “we-including-you walk”
- ordinary translation: “We walk.”

### `kənaxkoohəmaahna`

- analysis: `kə~naxkoohəmaa~hna`
- literal: “we-including-you sing”
- ordinary translation: “We sing.”

### `wəlew kəpəməsiihna`

- analysis: `wəlew kə~pəməsii~hna`
- literal: “goodwill, we-including-you walk”
- idiomatic continuity translation: “We walk well, with good hearts.”

Inclusive `we` is a deliberate performance interpretation: a communal chorus addresses and includes its listener. The exclusive form remains available when a speaker refers to a group that excludes the addressee.

This final clause is a provisional chorus kernel, not an accepted lyric. It preserves the song's ethical function through an explicit contact-language analysis, but still needs owner review and metrical testing against the melody.

## What remains open in the chorus

The rest of the four-line chorus is not being faked into completion:

- `aanay` and `or` cover literal “road” and “light,” but the road-gives-us-light relation and first-person plural object remain undesigned;
- the sourced hear and see stems are class-sensitive TA/TI predicates, so bare “we listen, we see” still needs an explicit absolute, intransitivizing, or object-suppression strategy;
- `kənaxkoohəmaahna` supplies “we sing,” but “sing brightness forward” still needs a licensed result/object relation and direction construction; and
- full chorus parallelism, coordinator behavior, stress, and meter remain later construction work.

Those gaps are recorded in the continuity ledger. They are the next construction targets, not invitations to infer grammar from the legacy endings.

## Evidence and honesty boundary

- BDB `לֵב²` supports a bounded Biblical Hebrew inner-disposition and moral-character range. It does not attest `wəlew` or the chorus clause.
- Academy Hebrew terminology and BDB `אוֹר²` support physical `אוֹר` “light.” They do not make “brightness forward” a lexical sense.
- O'Meara supports the cited Munsee analyses and the source clusivity cells. He does not attest the regularized contact forms.
- The songs protect artistic functions and helped set priorities. They do not attest source-language forms or the fictional contact history.

All new mixed forms and contact syntax are candid project design. Their quality must be judged by consistency, expressive usefulness, source-aware plausibility, and later human review—not by pretending they are recovered historical facts.
