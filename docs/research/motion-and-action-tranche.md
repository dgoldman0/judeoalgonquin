# Motion and action tranche

**Status:** manually authored candidate language design; nothing here is
canonical

**Level:** N1 novice enrichment

**Scope:** ordinary human motion, objectless drinking, narrowly objectful
finding, and a closed goal/source/route contrast

## Outcome

This tranche adds 45 structured records to the 180-record checkpoint corpus,
bringing the structured knowledge base to 225 noncanonical records. The new
records are seven source-facing lexemes, ten contact lexemes, five closed
constructions, three phrases, and twenty sentences. The language-bearing
choices were made through direct linguistic authorship and review by Codex;
scripts only validate declared records, dependencies, cells, and negative
boundaries. No generative API produced the vocabulary, paradigms, phrases, or
translations.

The work is intentionally deeper than a word list. It develops four motion
predicates across seven ordinary participants, develops an ordinary action to
the same seven-cell depth, adds one class-sensitive transitive action in four
carefully bounded cells, and composes three distinct spatial relations into
phrases and complete clauses. Every productive-looking pattern remains closed
to the cells printed here.

## Source evidence and contact-language decisions

Source-facing records preserve donor facts. They are research ingredients, not
finished Judeo-Algonquin words or constructions. Contact records are separate,
revision-pinned proposals and state every adaptation or semantic restriction.

### Ten contact lexical decisions

| Contact candidate | Layer | Deliberately admitted meaning | Deliberately excluded meaning or behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| `el` | direct contact inheritance | invariant spatial goal vector | arrival, entry, completion, telicity, TAM, donor inflection |
| `min` | direct contact inheritance | invariant physical source or reference center | tense, inception, completion, nonspatial Hebrew senses, donor allomorphy |
| `derex` | contact-native formation | invariant route relator before listed `aanay` | a donor-attested preposition, destination, manner, instrument, interior path, or metaphor |
| `aləməsii-` | direct contact inheritance | AI motion away from a contextual center | generic “go/come,” a built-in source NP, TAM, obviation, or narrative status |
| `kwaxkii-` | direct contact inheritance | animate participant returns or goes back | “power returns,” restoration, hurry, repetition, past time, or recurrence |
| `maačii-` | direct contact inheritance | animate participant goes home | “go to a house,” “go indoors,” “return,” possession, or an additional `bayit`/`bayitənk` goal |
| `mənee-` | direct contact inheritance | ordinary objectless drinking | alcohol-specific meaning, an overt beverage, productive `-ee`, habitual or completed aspect |
| `moxkaw-` | direct contact inheritance | TA find with the one admitted animate-class object | other persons, other objects, object omission, direct/inverse, searching or seeking |
| `moxkam-` | direct contact inheritance | TI find with the one admitted inanimate-class object | other persons, other objects, object omission, direct/inverse, fact discovery or realization |
| `delet` | direct contact inheritance | physical door; locally IN only inside the find construction | inherited Hebrew gender/plural, a general noun-class rule, possession, opening/closing, or static/directional behavior |

`derex` is the clearest formation distinction in the set. Biblical Hebrew
`derekh` supplies a physical-route noun; the contact language changes its
surface under the contact profile and grammaticalizes it as an invariant route
relator. The resulting function is an explicit project decision, not a false
claim about what BDB attests.

## Closed ordinary paradigms

### Twenty-eight motion cells

Four licensed AI stems take the already proposed transparent contact
person-number architecture. The table is exhaustive: four predicates times
seven participants, not a generator for other stems.

| Predicate | 1sg | 2sg | 3sg | 1pl inclusive | 1pl exclusive | 2pl | 3pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| walk `pəməsii-` | `nəpəməsiim` | `kəpəməsiim` | `pəməsiiw` | `kəpəməsiihna` | `nəpəməsiihna` | `kəpəməsiihmwa` | `pəməsiiwak` |
| go away `aləməsii-` | `nəaləməsiim` | `kəaləməsiim` | `aləməsiiw` | `kəaləməsiihna` | `nəaləməsiihna` | `kəaləməsiihmwa` | `aləməsiiwak` |
| return `kwaxkii-` | `nəkwaxkiim` | `kəkwaxkiim` | `kwaxkiiw` | `kəkwaxkiihna` | `nəkwaxkiihna` | `kəkwaxkiihmwa` | `kwaxkiiwak` |
| go home `maačii-` | `nəmaačiim` | `kəmaačiim` | `maačiiw` | `kəmaačiihna` | `nəmaačiihna` | `kəmaačiihmwa` | `maačiiwak` |

The contact morphography keeps the complete lexical stem visible. In
particular, `nə-` or `kə-` before vowel-initial `aləməsii-` retains transparent
`ə+a`; no source `t`-insertion is introduced. The generic source candidates
`aa-` “go” and `paa-` “come” remain class-unresolved and cannot enter this
paradigm. English simple-present renderings are glossing conveniences, not a
tense analysis.

### Seven objectless drink cells

| 1sg | 2sg | 3sg | 1pl inclusive | 1pl exclusive | 2pl | 3pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `nəməneem` | `kəməneem` | `məneew` | `kəməneehna` | `nəməneehna` | `kəməneehmwa` | `məneewak` |

All seven cells are intransitive in this tranche. A beverage cannot follow the
verb, and the paradigm provides no special alcohol, habitual, completed,
negative, imperative, or narrative reading.

### Four first-plural find cells

| Object class | Inclusive | Exclusive | Required overt object |
|---|---|---|---|
| animate | `kəmoxkawahna adam` | `nəmoxkawahna adam` | bare indefinite `adam` only |
| inanimate | `kəmoxkamohna delet` | `nəmoxkamohna delet` | bare indefinite `delet` only |

The TA `a` and TI `o` bridges are transparent contact abstractions from the
cited Absolute models; they are not claimed as free donor morphemes. The object
is mandatory, indefinite, bare, and class-compatible. It cannot be omitted,
made definite, possessed, pluralized, or replaced. This construction means
literal successful finding of an entity, not seeking, searching, realizing,
discovering a fact, or encoding intentionality. The legacy “foot found the
wet” line remains unlicensed.

## Three relators, three phrases, and three clauses

The relational layer distinguishes goal, source, and route. It licenses exactly
three phrase cells:

| Relation | Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| goal | `el bayit` | toward a house, without entailed arrival |
| source | `min bayit` | from a house as physical source or reference center |
| route | `derex aanay` | along or by way of a road |

Those phrases enter exactly three complete clause cells:

| Relation | Clause | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| goal | `yeled pəməsiiw el bayit` | a child walks toward a house |
| source | `yeled aləməsiiw min bayit` | a child goes away from a house |
| route | `adam pəməsiiw derex aanay` | a person walks along or by way of a road |

This is not a cross-product. The form is overt third-singular subject + licensed
third-singular motion predicate + invariant relator + bare listed complement.
`el`, `min`, and `derex` cannot be swapped across the three cells; arbitrary
nouns, pronouns, clause complements, and posture predicates remain outside the
construction.

The twenty sentence records also exercise otherwise unattested cells from the
motion and drink tables, both find classes and clusivities, and ordinary
coordination in `kəpəməsiihna wə-kəməneehna` “we, including you, walk and
drink.” That order states two coordinated ordinary activities; it does not
turn the first event into a narrative predecessor of the second.

## Semantic and grammatical firewalls

| Boundary | What the tranche does **not** license |
|---|---|
| Tense, aspect, modality | no past, future, completed, habitual, progressive, inceptive, modal, negative, or imperative value; English present is only a gloss |
| Narrative | no wayyiqtol-derived foreground chain, weqatal-derived consequence or recurring frame, event advancement, recurrence, consequence, or discourse-center shift; ordinary `wə-` is not narrative sequencing |
| Obviation and alignment | third-person cells are ordinary/default, not proximate or obviative; third plural does not recover multiple participants; find supplies no direct/inverse system |
| Static versus directional space | static `-ənk` cannot mark a goal, source, or route; `el bayitənk`, `min bayitənk`, and `derex aaneenk` are invalid; static `efo` cannot ask whither, whence, or route |
| Spatial roles | goal, source, and route remain distinct; none is a general preposition or case, and every complement is bare and host-listed |
| Motion lexemes | `aa-` and `paa-` remain unresolved; `kwaxkii-` requires an animate participant; lexical `maačii-` cannot take `bayit` or `bayitənk` |
| Valency | `mənee-` cannot take a beverage; find requires its exact overt indefinite object and cannot license the legacy foot/wet line |
| Productivity | no arbitrary stem inflection, noun replacement, nominal plurality, possession, static/directional extension, or general question system follows from these cells |

These boundaries protect the future hybrid narrative engine. An ordinary
third-person motion sentence is useful substrate for later narrative design,
but it cannot be relabeled as foreground, consequence, recurrence, or
proximate/obviative tracking without independently authored constructions.

## Creative-anchor continuity

The new system treats the preserved songs as active design constraints without
forcing their existing wording into canon.

- *We Walk Well* now has a full ordinary walk paradigm and a literal along-road
  clause, strengthening the communal and road-motion core. It still lacks a
  reviewed brightness-forward result, carrying and eating predicates,
  togetherness, lyric compression, and meter; route alone does not finish the
  song.
- *When the Lights Learn Our Names* now has ordinary animate return, a contact
  door, bounded finding, and clearer direction distinctions. These are useful
  scene ingredients, but `kwaxkii-` cannot mean that power returns, the legacy
  foot/wet line remains invalid, and ordinary clauses do not supply the
  required wayyiqtol–weqatal–obviation engine or its participant recovery.

The source song files remain unchanged. Continuity is partial and explicitly
bounded, not inferred merely because a related noun or verb now exists.

## Embedding checkpoint and cost boundary

Before these 45 records were added, the owner-authorized static-place semantic
checkpoint embedded all 180 records then present with
`text-embedding-3-small` at 256 dimensions. It sent 196 inputs—180 record
documents and 16 authored diagnostic queries—using 108,422 input tokens. The
successful provider call cost an estimated **$0.00216844**. Across four English,
four contact-language, four compositional, and four contrastive queries, the
diagnostic achieved recall@1 **0.6875**, recall@3 **0.9375**, and mean reciprocal
rank **0.8177083**, passing its predeclared recall@3 criteria.

That checkpoint is a retrieval diagnostic, not linguistic evidence, training,
canon review, or proof that a near neighbor is equivalent. Its sanitized report
is [Static-place semantic bootstrap](../reports/static-place-semantic-bootstrap-2026-07-13.json).
One failed network reservation remains conservatively counted in budget
accounting even though the stated dollar figure is the successful call's
estimated cost.

The 45 motion-and-action records were authored after that snapshot and have no
vectors in it. The structured corpus therefore has 225 records while the last
living semantic checkpoint covers 180. Paid embeddings are disabled, and no
refresh, live semantic query, or generative API call may be made without new
explicit owner approval. Exact structured lookup and deterministic validation
remain available locally.
