# Provenance and Cultural Policy

This policy applies to every lexical item, grammatical rule, translation, note, example, and cultural or historical statement in the project.

## 1. Three explanations that must remain separate

### Historical etymology

A historical etymology reports a supported history of a source form: the exact source language or lect, attested form, meaning, grammatical category, source citation, and any documented earlier stages.

It must not include a story invented to make a form feel ancient, culturally inevitable, or spiritually significant. If the historical chain is unknown, the field says so.

### Formation process

The formation process explains what this project did:

- direct borrowing;
- phonological or orthographic adaptation;
- calque;
- blend;
- compound;
- derivation under an accepted rule;
- semantic extension; or
- wholly new coinage.

It identifies each input and each transformation. This is project history, not source-language history.

### Design alignment

Design alignment explains why the proposed form fits the goals: register, source hierarchy, semantic fit, productivity, sound shape, contrast with existing forms, or usefulness in a paradigm.

It may be interpretive, but it must be labeled as a design judgment. It cannot assert that one source language uniquely owns spirituality, land, abstraction, ethics, literacy, emotion, or any other human domain.

## 2. Required evidence for a source-derived record

Before a source-derived entry can become canonical, it must record:

- exact language or lect;
- community or regional variety when the source provides one;
- source-form spelling exactly as cited;
- source meaning or meanings;
- part of speech and inflectional class where available;
- stable source ID from `references/sources.yaml`;
- page, entry URL, headword, recording, or other locator;
- access date for web material;
- source contributor or speaker attribution when published;
- license or reuse status;
- confidence and unresolved uncertainty; and
- the project's transformation from source form to proposed form.

“Algonquian,” “Native,” “Lenape/Munsee,” or “from the land” is not exact attribution.

## 3. Source priority and use

Community-published and speaker-connected material is preferred for identity, current usage, pronunciation, and community framing. Scholarly dictionaries and grammars remain important, especially where they were built with named speakers, but their publication and reuse terms still apply.

Munsee Delaware is the regional Algonquian anchor. Research looks first to responsible Mahican/Mohican and Lenape sources when Munsee documentation does not meet a real need. Broader Algonquian material—including explicitly labeled Algonquin, Ojibwe, Mi'kmaq, Penobscot, or other sources—may fill a documented gap after review. The record must preserve the exact origin and explain why the broader source was needed; adaptation never erases attribution.

Hebrew evidence may come from Modern/common, Biblical, Classical, rabbinic, liturgical, regional, or diaspora sources as the design requires. Records identify the relevant stratum. No policy confines Hebrew-derived words or morphology to abstract or elevated domains.

A source registry entry marked `verified` means only that the URL and publisher identity were checked on the recorded date. It does not mean:

- every statement on the site was independently verified;
- the material is licensed for bulk copying;
- a source from one lect applies to another; or
- culturally sensitive content is cleared for reuse.

Bulk extraction is prohibited until the source's terms permit it or permission is recorded.

## 4. Cultural and ceremonial material

Terms tied to ceremonies, medicines, sacred narratives, prayers, community offices, or restricted practices receive heightened review.

The default is not to generate or import such material. A candidate requires:

- a source connected to the relevant community;
- evidence that the material is appropriate for public use;
- a concrete reason it belongs in this constructed language;
- exact attribution without pan-Indigenous generalization; and
- human approval before it enters the candidate corpus, followed by separate human canon review.

The label `ritual_style` describes a constructed literary register. It must not imply that a generated text is an authentic Jewish, Munsee, Mahican/Mohican, Lenape, Algonquin, Ojibwe, or shared ceremony.

## 5. Fiction, history, and community identity

The project's foundational alternate history—a continuing Judea, an Atlantic-crossing Judean community, settlement in the Hudson/Mahicanituck Valley, and long development through pidgin and nativized-creole contact stages—is worldbuilding and must be identified as such in public presentation. The creole stage is understood as a full community language that continues to elaborate. Within that fictional setting, invented events and institutions remain fictional records. Real people, nations, treaties, publications, archaeological claims, and religious rulings may not be invented as background texture and presented as facts.

The project does not claim to speak for any source community. Community names, self-designations, and language names follow community sources where available. Historical exonyms and broad family labels are not silently substituted for self-identification.

## 6. Confidence and claim status

Every nontrivial claim has both a review status and an evidence confidence:

- `high` — directly supported by an exact, suitable source locator;
- `medium` — supported but subject to interpretation, dialect variation, or incomplete documentation;
- `low` — a lead or hypothesis that cannot support canon; or
- `unknown` — not yet researched.

Confidence does not replace review. A high-confidence source fact can still be unsuitable for this language; a well-designed coinage can be canonical while honestly having no historical etymology.

## 7. Citation practice

Citations should point to the most specific available location. Each paraphrase must remain faithful to the source. Quoted text is kept short and clearly marked.

If multiple sources disagree, the record stores the disagreement. The project does not erase it by selecting whichever spelling is easiest to fit into Hebrew script.

## 8. Inherited material

Repetition across inherited files is not independent evidence because many files were generated from one another. No inherited “etymology,” translation note, or language claim is carried forward without a new source review under this policy.

The two [creative-anchor songs](../references/creative-anchors/README.md) are a deliberate exception only in role: their existing wording, voice, imagery, themes, and ambitions remain active revision evidence. This does not exempt any individual language form, analysis, or historical claim from review.
